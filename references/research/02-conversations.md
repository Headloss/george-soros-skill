## 2. Reasoning patterns (live / dialogic)

### 2.1 Uncertainty and prediction hedging

- **Conditional, policy-dependent futures.** In the Moyers interview, Soros ties forecasts to policy response: “the one thing that my theory says is that you can't predict the future because the future depends on how you react to it.” **Direct** — PBS transcript (`transcript4.html` as listed).  
- **Unconditional predictions rejected.** In the Woodruff/NYRB interview, he states you cannot make *unconditional* predictions in human affairs because outcomes depend on how authorities respond; emphasizes imperfect understanding vs natural science. **Secondary** — NYRB print interview.  
- **Explicit strength-of-belief language.** BBC *Breakfast with Frost* (2001): describes US recovery path with hedges (“there is some question in my mind about the strength of the recovery”). **Direct** — BBC transcript.

**Inference:** In broadcast settings he often satisfies the interviewer’s demand for a forecast by embedding it in a scenario (“if we do the right things…”) rather than offering a single trajectory.

### 2.2 “Changing his mind” and self-revision narratives

- **Book vs event timing.** At the Dec 1998 CFR meeting he notes he began writing before the financial crisis and then incorporated developments as they unfolded—modeling intellectual revision as *integration of new facts* rather than flip-flopping. **Direct** (with hosting caveat) — CFR transcript copy.  
- **Open society ideal refined.** Macleans Q&A: describes discovering a “misconception” in his own Popperian ideal—critical thinking is not only cognitive (understanding reality) but manipulative (shaping perception); says he is not abandoning open society but adding a requirement. **Secondary** — GeorgeSoros.com/Macleans.  
- **CEU lectures (2009):** frames the financial crisis as validating/pushing him to articulate reflexivity publicly after prior neglect by economics (“failed philosopher” motif in lecture framing). **Direct** — OSF reflexivity transcript PDF.

**Contradiction preserved:** He argues for radical uncertainty and fallibility while also issuing strong crisis diagnoses (“worst since 1930s” class claims in 2008–09 media). The tension is *stated* in Woodruff (severity vs conditional prediction) and in Moyers (height of crisis vs hope authorities adapt).

### 2.3 Markets, politics, history — recurring causal frames

- **Bubble decomposition** (invariant across years): a real trend + a misconception that initially reinforces both, eventually overshoots and collapses. **Direct** — CFR Mallaby 2008; CNN Zakaria 2008 rush transcript; overlaps NYRB Woodruff.  
- **“Market fundamentalism”** as label for equilibrium faith and hands-off policy. **Direct** — Moyers; CNN Zakaria; Macleans.  
- **Reflexivity / fallibility** as twin axioms in lecture mode: distorted views of participants; views affect situations via inappropriate actions; feedback loops. **Direct** — OSF CEU transcript PDF (definitions and examples such as drug policy, “government is bad”).  
- **Geopolitical economy:** dollar reserve status, US external imbalances, global regulation harder than deregulatory “contagion.” **Direct** — BBC Davos 2010 Peston transcript.  
- **Philanthropy as parallel to state aims, not identity with it.** NPR 2005: Open Society shares objectives with State Department but differs in method; stands by “conscience of the world” line for foundations, not personal aggrandizement. **Direct** — NPR transcript.

### 2.4 Questions dodged, reframed, or bounded

- **Declines market-moving specificity.** Peston (Davos 2010): “I abstain from making these comments because I don't want to influence the markets” re sterling/gilts, while still discussing UK structural problems on record. **Direct** — BBC transcript.  
- **Declines political office / role speculation.** Zakaria (Oct 2008 rush): “No. I'm a little bit old… I would certainly be available with advice… But I'm not going to …” — truncates Treasury secretary line. **Direct** — CNN transcript.  
- **Humor / meta on caricature.** Zakaria 2011 transcript (GeorgeSoros.com): responds to Glenn Beck puppet-master clip with amusement and frame reversal (“projecting…”) and tells audience they heard “the puppet master” so they can decide. **Direct** — first-party transcript.  
- **“Not advising China” while advising.** Peston exchange: gives analysis of renminbi appreciation then says difficult to advise Chinese because they resent it — “So I am not advising them. I am just telling you.” **Direct** — BBC Davos 2010. **Inference:** rhetorical separation of audience (Western public) from addressee (Chinese policymakers).

### 2.5 Tone and epistemic posture

- **Self-deprecating opener under time pressure.** CFR 1998: won’t stand; “impossible task” to summarize book in ten minutes. **Direct** — CFR transcript copy.  
- **Admits prior analytical failures in macro teaching context.** Kaletsky dialog (*Business Economics*): references teaching macro in 1970s without understanding it; positions INET as response to theoretical “boom-bust of ideas.” **Secondary** — DOI article.  
- **Moral-philosophical closure in interviews.** Moyers ties personal history (fascism, communism) to rejection of false certainties and to “market fundamentalism” as a third false certainty. **Direct** — PBS.

## 4. Signs of viewpoint evolution (conversation-visible)

**Note:** This section tracks *stated* shifts or re-balancings in dialog, not net worth of his written corpus.

1. **1998 → 2008–10:** Greater emphasis on *regulatory redesign* and *global institutional innovation* (“new Bretton Woods”, SDRs) in post-crisis interviews (Peston) vs late-90s book launch focused on global capitalism’s structural bias and need for global *society* (CFR 1998 opening themes). **Direct** — CFR 1998 transcript copy; BBC 2010.

3. **INET founding stance (New Scientist Q&A):** funds without imposing reflexivity; stay under 1/3 funding; not on boards — presents institutional humility distinct from “guru imposes worldview.” **Secondary** — *New Scientist*.

## 5. Candidate heuristics (for skill design — each needs live-testing against new transcripts)

1. **Two-component bubble scan:** For any boom narrative, separate (a) verifiable trend from (b) simplifying belief; ask which feedback loop links them. **Evidence basis:** **Direct** — CFR 2008, CNN 2008.  
2. **Policy-contingent forecasting rule:** When asked “what happens next,” first classify levers (authorities, market participants) then give branches. **Evidence basis:** **Direct** — Moyers; **Secondary** — Woodruff NYRB.  
3. **Regime labeling:** Name the dominant *ideology* (e.g. market fundamentalism) to explain delayed intervention. **Evidence basis:** **Direct** — Moyers, CNN, Macleans.  
4. **Boundary maintenance on influence:** Refuse trade ideas that move markets; separate analysis for public good from order placement. **Evidence basis:** **Direct** — BBC Davos 2010. **Inference:** heuristic may be context-dependent (hedge fund era vs later).  
5. **Open society stress test:** Ask whether speech acts are primarily cognitive (seeking truth) or manipulative (seeking power); treat adversarial politics as reflexivity case. **Evidence basis:** **Secondary** — Macleans.  
6. **Institutional design humility:** When backing institutions, disclaim personal control (INET pattern). **Evidence basis:** **Secondary** — *New Scientist*.
