## 2. Recurring core arguments (≥3 appearances in corpora above)

Each bullet: **Claim → where it recurs → confidence.**

1. **Fallibility:** In situations with thinking participants, views of the world are partial/distorted; theories/overall pictures are biased or inconsistent. — *OSF Reflexivity transcript*; *OSF Open Society transcript* (fallibility links pillars); *JEM 2013 / georgesoros.com*. — **High** (verbatim repetition of “two propositions” structure).

2. **Reflexivity:** Distorted or imperfect views influence the situation through action; feedback between thinking and affairs is central; economics neglects this in favor of equilibrium/certainty. — *OSF Reflexivity*; *JEM 2013*; 1994 speech text (blog) uses two functions and feedback. — **High** for first two; **Medium** for 1994 layer (secondary host).

3. **Twin functions of participants’ thinking:** (a) understanding reality vs (b) affecting it. **Terminology shift:** 1994 text uses **cognitive** vs **participating** function (*Ritholtz* republication); 2009–2013 uses **cognitive** vs **manipulative** function (*JEM 2013*). — **High** that both pairs name the same structural idea; **High** that labels differ across time.

4. **Sharp critique of mainstream economics:** Equilibrium, rational expectations, efficient markets, and “physics envy” misdescribe social reality; social science should not expect Newtonian laws. — *OSF Reflexivity* (equilibrium vs reflexivity); *JEM 2013* (extended: Robbins, Hayek/Popper exchange, EMH, market fundamentalism). — **High**.

5. **Financial markets as laboratory:** Prices distort fundamentals and can change future fundamentals; positive vs negative feedback; boom–bust “bubble” anatomy; far-from-equilibrium vs near-equilibrium. — *JEM 2013* (detailed); *OSF Reflexivity* (preview/bridge to next lecture). — **High** for *JEM 2013*.

6. **Open society tied to fallibility and (for Popper) epistemic humility:** No final possession of truth; institutional preference for democracy/free thought as response to imperfect knowledge; Soros extends with philanthropy and later political diagnosis. — *OSF Reflexivity* (Popper, personal history); *OSF Open Society* (Popper’s lack of tight definition, epistemological argument); *Fight of Our Lives*; *Polycrisis*. — **High**.

7. **Open vs closed society — operational contrast (late Soros):** “In an open society, the role of the state is to protect[/defend] the freedom of the individual; in a closed society, the role of the individual is to serve the rulers of the state.” — *Fight of Our Lives* (Project Syndicate); *Polycrisis* (georgesoros.com). — **High** (near-identical wording).

8. **Super-bubble / crisis narrative:** Reflexivity and policy/market interaction explain major modern crises; 2008 as turning point for reception of his ideas. — *OSF Reflexivity*; *JEM 2013* (super-bubble, euro crisis section); author’s intro cites his 2008–2012 books. — **High**.

## 5. Evolution / tensions in his thinking (preserved, not flattened)

1. **Vocabulary:** **Participating** (1994 speech text) → **Manipulative** (2009–2013+). **Interpretation:** same dual role of thought; later term stresses intention. — **Medium** (inference on sameness); labels **High** as documented.

2. **Open society theory:** From **Popperian epistemic defense** toward explicit worry that **manipulative politics** and **propaganda** undermine the assumption that free discourse tracks truth — OSF *Open Society* transcript (Bush re-election, Luntz, consumer society). — **High**.

3. **“Original” emphasis:** Claims **misconceptions** shaping history as his distinctive emphasis beyond Popper — OSF *Open Society* transcript. — **High** (first-party).

4. **Methodological convention:** Proposes renouncing strict **unity of science** in favor of recognizing social-science limits — then **postscript** (*JEM 2013*) warns sharp separation could **harm long-term**; spectrum view preferred — **High** (explicit self-revision).

5. **Reception narrative:** Long period of treating his philosophy as “failed” / ignored academically → 2008 as reputational and intellectual turning point — *OSF Reflexivity*; *JEM 2013* intro. — **High**.

6. **Contemporary geopolitics & technology:** AI as asymmetric tool aiding **closed** societies and surveillance; undercuts simple faith in “reality” as moral anchor — *Polycrisis*; *Fight of Our Lives*. — **High** for texts; **Medium** for how far this updates vs replaces earlier epistemology (**inference**: extension of manipulative-function dominance theme).

## 7. Notable quotes (short; with attribution)

> “I can state the core idea in two relatively simple propositions. One is that in situations that have thinking participants, the participants' view of the world is always partial and distorted. That is the principle of fallibility. The other is that these distorted views can influence the situation to which they relate because false views lead to inappropriate actions. That is the principle of reflexivity.”  
> — George Soros, *General Theory of Reflexivity* transcript (OSF, 2009). **Confidence: high**

> “If there is anything really original in my thinking it is this emphasis on misconceptions. It provides a strong argument in favor of critical thinking and open society.”  
> — George Soros, *Open Society* transcript (OSF, 2009). **Confidence: high**

> “The two principles are tied together like Siamese twins, but fallibility is the firstborn: without fallibility there would be no reflexivity.”  
> — George Soros, *JEM* 2013 / georgesoros.com. **Confidence: high**

> “I refer to the two systems of governance as open and closed societies. I define the difference between the two as follows: in an open society, the role of the state is to defend the freedom of the individual; in a closed society, the role of the individual is to serve the interests of the rulers.”  
> — George Soros, “Can Democracy Survive the Polycrisis?” (georgesoros.com, 2023). **Confidence: high**

> “In an open society, the role of the state is to protect the freedom of the individual; in a closed society, the role of the individual is to serve the rulers of the state.”  
> — George Soros, “The Fight of Our Lives” (Project Syndicate, 2022). **Confidence: high** (note “protect” vs “defend” wording variant)

> “Strictly speaking, both interpretations are pseudo-scientific by Popper’s standards. That is why I called my first book ‘The Alchemy of Finance’.”  
> — George Soros, *JEM* 2013 / georgesoros.com. **Confidence: high**
