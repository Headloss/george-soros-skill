### 2. Black Wednesday — short sterling / ERM break (16 September 1992)

| Dimension | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Background** | UK in ERM; political pressure after Danish Maastricht referendum; sterling trading near lower band; Bundesbank tightness vs. UK macro weakness (secondary synthesis). |
| **Thesis / logic** | **Secondary account:** Sterling peg unsustainable at prevailing rates / policy mix; large downside if peg breaks — standard macro narrative in financial press and summaries. **Direct (Soros framework, not trade-specific):** reflexivity and mispricing of official commitments appear repeatedly in Soros’s later methodological writings. |
| **Execution** | Quantum built a very large short sterling position (popular reporting often cites ~$10bn notional context); Bank of England raised rates and intervened; UK exited the ERM. |
| **Outcome** | Sharp sterling depreciation; Soros/Quantum widely reported to have made on the order of ~$1bn (magnitude varies by outlet and currency). |
| **Retrospective** | **Secondary — Druckenmiller speech (Jan 2015):** Stanley Druckenmiller recounts Soros encouraging far larger sizing than Druckenmiller initially proposed (“200% of our net worth” language in transcript summaries). This is **not** Soros’s own contemporaneous trading memo — treat as **Secondary / participant recall — Medium** for exact wording and sizing. |

**Sources & confidence**  
- **Secondary — Medium–High:** Wikipedia, “Black Wednesday” (timeline, policy mechanics). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Wednesday  
- **Secondary — Medium:** Investopedia explainer (popular mechanics; check figures against primary accounts). https://www.investopedia.com/ask/answers/08/george-soros-bank-of-england.asp  
- **Secondary — Medium:** *The New York Times* Dealbook (2011) recap embeds the $10bn bet framing in broader Soros profile context. https://archive.nytimes.com/dealbook.nytimes.com/2011/07/26/soros-to-close-his-fund-to-outsiders/index.html  
- **Secondary — Medium (retrospective execution detail):** ValueWalk summary of Druckenmiller Lost Tree Club remarks. https://www.valuewalk.com/stan-druckenmiller-lost-tree-club/  
- **Direct — High (methodology, not the trade):** Soros, “Fallibility, reflexivity, and the human uncertainty principle,” *Journal of Economic Methodology* (2013), DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/1350178X.2013.859415  
- **Direct — High (methodology):** OSF-hosted lecture transcript, “General Theory of Reflexivity” (CEU, 26 Oct 2009). https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/uploads/9ae17912-2262-4646-8ffc-d01afc934c36/george-soros-general-theory-of-reflexivity-transcript.pdf  

**Contradiction / tension to preserve**  
- Public legend: Soros single-handedly “broke” the Bank of England. **Secondary synthesis:** Many actors shorted sterling; official intervention failed for macro/policy reasons — Soros was the most visible concentrated player. **Confidence: Medium** (standard in academic/post-mortem literature; see Black Wednesday secondary sources).

## V. Lessons and persistent tensions

1. **Trader vs. reformer:** Profit from disorder vs. philanthropic mission to reduce injustice — not logically impossible, but **politically charged**; Soros acknowledges market dangers while trading (**Direct** 1997; **Inference** on reputation).  
2. **Causal attribution in crises:** Asian crisis shows **gap** between **market participant role** and **blame narratives** (Mahathir vs Soros).  
3. **Size vs. alpha:** 2000 and 2011 decisions both reference **scale / regulation / instability** constraining the original hedge-fund model — **Secondary High**.  
4. **Institutional persistence:** Move from personal trading genius to **bureaucratic philanthropy** creates **legitimacy vs. conspiracy** dynamics — **Inference — Medium** (supported indirectly by AP expert quotes Jun 2023).  
5. **Succession contradiction:** Anti-nepotism **principle** vs. **familial chair** — **Secondary High** (CNBC/WSJ).
